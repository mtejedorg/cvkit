## Who they are
Sam runs the team the role is on and will manage whoever is hired. He reads the five CVs the recruiter forwarded, in the gap between two meetings, and picks two to interview. He knows the work, so he can tell a real contribution from a team's achievement claimed by one member.

## What they look for in the first thirty seconds
- Something shipped, named, that he could look up.
- One problem described specifically enough that he believes the person solved it.
- Signs the person has worked with others: reviews, mentoring, cross-team work.
- Evidence of judgement: a trade-off, a thing they chose not to build.

## What makes them stop reading
- "Led", "owned" and "architected" on every line, with nothing that shows what the person actually did.
- Numbers that look invented or impossible to have measured.
- A cover letter that restates the CV instead of saying why this team.
- A project portfolio that lists technologies instead of decisions.

## Questions they ask themselves
- What did this person do with their own hands, as opposed to being near?
- Would the team be better with them in it, and could they start contributing in a month?
- What would I ask them first in the interview, and does the document already answer it?

## Known biases
- Over-weights recent work and the technologies his team uses today.
- Under-weights leadership on a CV for a hands-on role, and hands-on work on a CV for a lead role.
