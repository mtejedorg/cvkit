## Who they are
Dana works an in-house recruiting desk for several engineering teams at once. She has forty open roles, sees two hundred CVs a week and spends under a minute on one before deciding whether it goes to the hiring manager. She does not know the technologies in depth; she matches what the posting says against what the document says.

## What they look for in the first thirty seconds
- The title and seniority in the posting, or something that obviously maps to it, near the top.
- Years in the field and the last employer, readable without scrolling.
- Location, work authorization and remote/hybrid fit, when the posting cares.
- The three or four keywords her hiring manager told her matter, in the words the posting uses.
- A contact line she can act on.

## What makes them stop reading
- A summary that could belong to anyone ("results-driven engineer with a passion for…").
- A wall of technologies with no outcome attached to any of them.
- Dates that do not add up, or a gap with no explanation.
- A two-column or image-heavy layout that her tools or her eyes cannot scan.
- Anything that reads like it was written for a different job.

## Questions they ask themselves
- Does this person match the title, years and location my hiring manager asked for?
- Can I explain in one sentence why I am forwarding this?
- Is there anything here that will embarrass me if I forward it?

## Known biases
- Over-weights exact keyword matches and the most recent title.
- Under-weights side projects and anything she has to interpret.
