# Posts and messages

## Posts

A post has 3,000 characters, but only about the first 210 show before "see more", so the first line decides whether anyone reads the rest. Write that line last.

**Shape that works without tricks**

1. Hook, one line: the concrete thing, not the lesson. "The chicken steals your food before the packet confirms it. Here is why that was the right call."
2. Context, two or three short lines: what the project is, where it stands (the manifest's `status`), what you did.
3. The substance, three to six short paragraphs or a short list: the decision, the problem, the number if the source has one, what you would do again.
4. One closing line that invites a reply on the substance, not "agree?".
5. At most three hashtags, after a blank line, or none.

Keep it between 600 and 1,300 characters unless the content earns more. Line breaks between paragraphs; no walls.

**Sources**

A post cites its facts the way a CV does. Acceptable sources: a project manifest (`portfolio.json`), the repo's README or design docs, a CV folder in `_generated/`, the profile's `verified metrics`, or an event the user describes. Each variant ends with a "facts used" block listing source lines, which stays in the draft file and never in the post.

**Images**

One real capture beats a carousel of generated art. Suggest a specific file (a project's `preview.jpg`, a capture from `Captures/`) and say why. Never suggest or produce AI-generated imagery for a post.

**Links**

The platform's treatment of external links shifts; the draft says where the link goes (in the post or in the first comment) and leaves the choice to the user.

**Building in public with AI agents**

The author wants AI-assisted work visible. Say what the agents did in that piece of work in one plain sentence ("Claude Code wrote the first pass of the network sync; I rewrote the authority rules") and what you did. Specific beats "powered by AI".

## Messages

| Kind | Length | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Connection note | ≤ 300 characters | Who you are in half a line, why this person, one sentence of substance, no ask beyond connecting. |
| Reply to a recruiter | 3–6 short lines | Thank, answer their actual question, state the one thing that matters (availability, location rule from the profile, the strongest proof point), propose a next step with two time options. |
| Follow-up after applying | 3–5 lines | Name the role and the date applied, one proof point that fits the posting, one question. Once; a second follow-up only after two weeks. |
| Thank-you after interview | 4–6 lines | One specific thing from the conversation, one point you want to add or correct, availability for next steps. Sent the same day. |
| Recommendation request | 5–8 lines | What you worked on together, the two things you would like mentioned (from `## Proof points`), an offer to write one back. |

Rules: first person, their name once, no templates that read as templates, no phone number unless the user says so. Location and relocation follow the profile's `rule:` line (for the author: do not lead with it).
